# Clipzy walkthrough — voiceover script

For `public/demo/walkthrough.mp4` (1:23, silent).

Target pace is about 2.6 words per second, which is relaxed narration, not
rushed. Every line below is written to fit its shot with room to breathe. Times
are where each shot starts, so land the first word just after it.

---

### 0:00 — Sign-in screen, Twitch auth, clips load *(14 words)*

> This is Clipzy. You sign in with Twitch, and your own clips load automatically.

### 0:06 — Swiping keep and archive *(21 words)*

> Newest first, so you start with last night. Watch one, keep it or archive it,
> and the next one's already playing.

### 0:17 — Library, sort and filter *(20 words)*

> Everything you keep lands in your library. Sort by newest, oldest, or view
> count. Filter by what you've already exported.

### 0:26 — Search *(14 words)*

> If you remember a moment but not when it happened, just search for it.

### 0:32 — Clip details, still a cloud clip *(14 words)*

> Open one up. At this point it's still on Twitch, not on your phone.

### 0:38 — Download, status flips to Ready to edit *(19 words)*

> One tap pulls it down. Now it's yours offline, and editing is unlocked. That's
> a one-time thing per clip.

### 0:47 — Editor opens on the safe-zone preview *(17 words)*

> Here's the editor. Your clip is horizontal, TikTok and Shorts are vertical, so
> something has to give.

### 0:55 — Layout sheet, Full vs Stacked *(20 words)*

> Full drops the whole frame in. Stacked splits it, so your face-cam goes up top
> and the gameplay sits underneath.

### 1:05 — Captions generating *(13 words)*

> Captions come from the clip's own audio, generated right here on the phone.

### 1:11 — Caption editing and styles *(27 words)*

> Then fix anything it misheard, retime it, pick a style, and burn them in. After
> that you export, and it's in your camera roll ready to post.

---

**Total: 179 words over 83 seconds**, roughly 129 words per minute. That is
deliberately below conversational speed because the viewer is reading the screen
at the same time.

## Delivery notes

- Hard stop after "…already playing", which lands around 0:16. That beat is the
  whole product and it deserves a second of silence before the library starts.
- "One tap pulls it down" at 0:38 is the line people misunderstand. Slow down.
- Don't sell. Every line describes what is visibly happening, so let the screen
  carry it and stay flat and matter-of-fact.
- The roomiest beats are 0:06 and 0:55, both around 120 words per minute. If you
  finish early there, hold the silence rather than padding.

## Two things the script does not cover

The export itself is not on screen — the video ends in the caption editor. The
last line says "after that you export" for that reason. If you want export shown,
that needs a fresh capture.

There is also no cold open. If you want a hook before 0:00, record this over a
held first frame and shift everything later:

> Every streamer has a hundred clips they've never looked at again. This is what
> I built to fix mine.
